P1

PS1, Part A, Q9 (comprehension).

Explain how the truth-table statement (P ∧ Q) ⇒ Q is used to prove A ∩ B ⊆ B. What do P and Q stand for?

P2

PS1, Part B, Q4(a)-(c).

(a) Use a truth table to show P ⇒ (P ∨ Q) is always true. (b) Use it to show A ⊆ A ∪ B. (c) Use it to show that x < y implies x ≤ y for real x,y.

P3

Selected parts of PS1, Part A, Q5.

Identify A8={x∈Z: ∀k∈Z, x=2k}, A9={x∈Z: ∃k∈Z, x=2k}, and A10={x∈Z: ∃k∈R, x=2k}. Then compare A6={x∈Z: x≥1 or x≤3} with A7={x∈Z: x≥1 and x≤3}.

P4 · Optional

Tutor-written extension: set equality using logical equivalence.

Let A,B,C be subsets of X. Prove A ∩ (B ∪ C) = (A ∩ B) ∪ (A ∩ C) by translating membership into logical statements.

P5

PS1, Part B, Q2. Explain your reasons as an extra classroom activity.

Decide which are true over R: (a) ∀x ∃y: xy=1; (b) ∀x ∀y: xy=1; (c) ∃x ∃y: xy=1; (d) ∃x ∀y: xy=1.

P6

Tutor-written extension: negation of quantified statements.

Let A,B be subsets of X. Negate (i) ∀x∈R ∃y∈R: xy=1; (ii) ∀x∈X: x∈A ⇒ x∈B. Then negate “x∈A and x∈B” and “x∈A or x∈B”.
