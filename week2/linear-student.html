2 · Three legal elementary row operations

Swap two different rows; multiply a row by a non-zero scalar; add a multiple of a different row to a row. Each operation is reversible and preserves the solution set. Apply it to the ENTIRE augmented row, including the right-hand side. Multiplying a row by zero is not a legal elementary operation.

3 · Leading entries and pivots

The leading entry is the first non-zero entry from the left in a non-zero row. In echelon form these are the pivots, and their locations are pivot positions. A zero row has no leading entry. Not every 1 in a matrix is a pivot.

4 · REF versus RREF

REF means row echelon form: zero rows are at the bottom; leading positions move strictly right as you move down the non-zero rows, so entries below pivots are zero. THIS COURSE also requires each leading entry to be 1. Some textbooks allow other non-zero leading entries in REF. RREF means reduced row echelon form: in addition, each leading 1 is the only non-zero entry in its column, including above it. Every RREF matrix is also REF; not every column needs a pivot.

5 · Read the solution type: check consistency FIRST

After row reduction of an augmented matrix over the real numbers: a row [0 … 0 | c] with c ≠ 0 means no solution. If there is no contradiction, a pivot in every variable column means a unique solution; at least one free variable means infinitely many solutions. The right-hand-side column is not a variable column. A zero row by itself does NOT prove there are infinitely many solutions. A free variable corresponds to a variable column without a pivot in echelon form.
